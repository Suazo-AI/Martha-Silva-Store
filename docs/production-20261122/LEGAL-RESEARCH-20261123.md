## 1. Prices in USD or córdobas

### Answer

Ley 732 Art. 36 says prices and obligations paid in Nicaragua are expressed and settled in córdobas.
Ley 732 Art. 37 lists nine exceptions.
Exception 1 covers obligations in a foreign currency that can be converted to córdobas at an exchange rate fixed or determinable at the time of payment.
Exception 9 covers any other case the BCN board authorizes.
Ley 842 Art. 10(4) forbids charging more than the shown price.
Art. 10(4) also says shown prices include taxes "expresados en moneda nacional".
Art. 10(4) lets only "personas proveedoras con regulación especial" show prices in dollars.
Ley 842 Art. 23(4) requires the price "en moneda nacional a la vista del público", with taxes included.
Ley 842 Art. 10(5) forbids charging or invoicing in a foreign currency.
Art. 10(5) exempts free-zone companies, bonded warehouses and "demás excepciones establecidas en el Artículo 37" of Ley 732.
Ley 842 Art. 10(6) forbids using a rate below the BCN official rate of the day when the buyer chooses to pay in a foreign currency.
Reglamento 842 Art. 6 lets certified tourist restaurants show indicative dollar prices on menus, paid in córdobas at the official rate.
Reading Art. 6 as an example of "regulación especial" is our inference.
Label: official text read for every article in this section.

### The tension

Art. 37 enters Ley 842 only through Art. 10(5), which is about charging and invoicing.
Art. 10(4) and Art. 23(4), which are about showing prices, have no Art. 37 cross-reference.
So Art. 37(1) may allow a USD-referenced sale, but the display rules still ask for córdobas.
This is our reading, not a ruling.
We found no special regulation that covers a clothing store.

### Options for the owner

| Option | Our reading |
|---|---|
| Córdobas with taxes as the main price; USD optional as a reference | Safe under every reading |
| USD with the córdoba equivalent next to it, at the BCN official rate, taxes included | Depends on a lawyer; córdobas are shown, but as the secondary figure |
| USD only (today) | Weakest; conflicts with the text of Art. 10(4) and 23(4) |

## 2. Registration of the data file (Ley 787 Art. 22)

### Answer, in separate claims

The duty: Ley 787 Art. 22 says every file controller must register in the "Registro de ficheros de datos" that the Dirección de Protección de Datos Personales enables.
Art. 22 also says the controller waits 30 days for a decision.
Label: official text read.
The authority: Ley 787 Art. 28 creates that Dirección inside the Ministerio de Hacienda y Crédito Público (MHCP).
Art. 30 says the Dirección "habilitará" the registry.
Label: official text read.
Whether it operates: Lex Mundi (2025) says the authority "is not yet organized, so there is no active regulator".
Label: secondary source.
A procedure: no registration form, fee, office or contact was found.
Label: not found.
Our MHCP check covered the hacienda.gob.ni homepage only; it did not mention personal data.

### What a small store can do now (inference)

Do not write "registered" or "registration not needed" on the site.
Keep an internal record with the Art. 22 fields (a) to (h).
Those fields are: controller name and address; kind of data; how and when data are collected; recipients; how data are linked; security measures and who handles the data; retention time; how buyers ask for access, correction or deletion.
Keep the privacy notice the site already has.
Ley 787 Art. 7 and Reglamento 787 Art. 14-15 ask for a plain notice before collecting data.
If the registry opens, the record is ready to file.

## 3. Withdrawal right for distance sales

### Answer

Ley 842 Art. 81: in a distance sale the buyer may cancel within five business days from receiving the item.
Art. 81 leaves the "form" and the "exceptions" to the Reglamento.
Reglamento 842 Art. 41: in a distance sale the buyer may withdraw only if the item does not match the offer or has a defect.
Reglamento 842 Art. 42: withdrawal is valid by sending a withdrawal notice or by returning the goods.
Reglamento 842 Art. 40(1) excludes goods that cannot be resold once used or taken out of their packaging.
Reglamento 842 Art. 40(2) excludes made-to-measure goods if the seller proves they were already made when the notice arrived.
Under Art. 40(2), if the goods are only partly made, the buyer may still withdraw by paying for the work done.
Art. 40(2) also asks the seller to warn the buyer of this limit in writing.
Art. 40 is titled for sales "fuera del establecimiento"; applying it to online sales is our inference.
Ley 842 Art. 82(5) says the seller must tell the buyer the withdrawal terms.
Label: official text read for Art. 40, 41, 42, 81 and 82.

The live page already promises the broader version: five business days, no condition.
The live page also promises a one-month return window, "reviewed case by case".
If the one-month policy is real, version B changes little for buyers in practice.

### Clause drafts (identical text in the owner form)

Version A, follows the law:

> Podés retractarte de tu compra, sin dar motivo, dentro de los cinco días hábiles siguientes a recibir la prenda. La prenda debe estar sin usar y con sus etiquetas. Avisanos por WhatsApp o por escrito, o devolvenos la prenda. Te devolvemos lo pagado.

Added to version A only if the store sells made-to-measure dresses online:

> Si la prenda se hace a tu medida y ya está terminada cuando nos avisás, este derecho no aplica. Si está a medio hacer, podés retractarte pagando lo ya hecho. Te lo avisamos por escrito antes de que pagues.

Tradeoff: matches the law and today's page, but the store accepts returns without a reason.
The "sin usar y con sus etiquetas" condition comes from Art. 40(1) by inference.

Version B, follows the Reglamento:

> Si la prenda que recibís no coincide con lo ofrecido o tiene un defecto, podés retractarte dentro de los cinco días hábiles siguientes a recibirla. Avisanos por WhatsApp o por escrito, o devolvenos la prenda. Te devolvemos lo pagado.

Tradeoff: fewer returns, but a Reglamento ranks below the law, so an authority may hold that the broader right still applies.
